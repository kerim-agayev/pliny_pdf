iLovePDF is one of the most-used PDF toolkits on the web, and it earned that position honestly: a huge range of tools, a clean interface, and reliable results. If you need OCR, PDF-to-Excel, or a dozen niche conversions in one place, it delivers.

But it has one characteristic that matters more every year: **it uploads your files to its servers** to do almost everything, including operations that don't need a server at all. If you've become more careful about where your documents go, it's worth knowing the alternatives. Here's a fair comparison.

## What to actually compare

"Best" depends on what you value. The dimensions that matter:

- **Where processing happens** — your browser (local) or their server (cloud)?
- **Tool coverage** — how many operations, and the ones you actually use?
- **Privacy posture** — retention, tracking, and whether files train models.
- **Price** — and what the free tier really allows.

## iLovePDF (the baseline)

**Strengths:** Very broad tool set, OCR, strong brand, polished UX, mobile apps.

**Trade-offs:** Server-side by default — your files are uploaded even for simple tasks like merging or rotating. Free tier has daily task limits and pushes hard toward a subscription. If privacy or jurisdiction is a concern, "uploaded and deleted later" is the model you're accepting.

Good fit if: you need maximum tool breadth and aren't sensitive about uploads.

## Smallpdf

**Strengths:** Beautiful interface, smooth workflows, good integrations (Google Drive, Dropbox).

**Trade-offs:** Also cloud-first — files are uploaded. The free tier is quite restrictive (limited daily operations), and it's one of the more expensive options once you hit the paywall. You're paying a premium largely for polish.

Good fit if: you want the slickest UX and don't mind paying for it.

## BentoPDF

**Strengths:** Genuinely local — many tools run in your browser, which is a real privacy win. Open and lightweight.

**Trade-offs:** Narrower scope. No AI features, and no high-fidelity PDF-to-Word conversion (the kind of task that genuinely needs a server). The interface is functional rather than premium.

Good fit if: you want local processing and your needs stay within its tool set.

## PlinyPDF

**Strengths:** Local-first by design — ten of its thirteen tools run entirely in your browser via WebAssembly, so files for merge, split, compress, rotate, watermark, password, and editing never leave your machine. You can verify this in DevTools. It also offers the things pure-local tools skip: high-fidelity [PDF to Word](/pdf-to-word) and an [AI summary](/summarize) tool — clearly labeled as cloud, processed over an encrypted connection, deleted within 24 hours, and never used for training. Full interface in English, Turkish, and Russian. No ads, no tracking, generous free tier.

**Trade-offs:** Fewer total tools than iLovePDF (thirteen focused tools rather than a hundred). No OCR or PDF-to-Excel yet. It's newer, so the brand is less established.

Good fit if: you want local processing for everyday tasks *plus* the option of cloud conversion and AI when you actually need them — with honesty about which is which.

## How to choose

Start with the question that actually decides it: **do you care where your files are processed?**

- If you don't, iLovePDF or Smallpdf give you the broadest, most polished experience.
- If you do, the choice is between a pure-local tool like BentoPDF and a local-first hybrid like PlinyPDF. The deciding factor is whether you ever need server-only features (Word conversion, AI). If you don't, either works. If you do, the hybrid saves you from juggling two tools.

## A note on "free"

Watch what "free" funds. If a tool is free and shows ads or has trackers, your attention and data are part of the deal. If it's free with a clearly optional paid tier and no ads, the free experience is the product, and the paid tier is the business. The second model tends to age better for users.

## The bottom line

iLovePDF is a fine tool that happens to upload everything. In 2026, that's an increasingly conscious trade-off rather than a default. If you want to keep your files on your own machine for the tasks that allow it, try the local-first approach — [run any PlinyPDF tool](/tools) with the DevTools Network tab open and watch the uploads that never happen.
